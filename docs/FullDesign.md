## The game

ROSE is an extraction colony-builder. You send Runners up into the dark for loot and bodies, haul it back to a city that lives on a purple ooze network, decide item by item what gets sold, fed, crafted or kept, and spend the surplus to climb six strata to Night City before the Oathkeepers burn you out. The city runs itself on laws you sign while you are away on a run; the run is where you can lose everything you carried.

**The master loop**, one cycle of which is 20–40 minutes:

1. **Prepare** at the Heart: pick a route, a Runner, two followers, pack the rig from the Stash.
2. **Run** (real time, 8–15 min): unlit tunnels, containers, deposits, bodies, ferals, patrols, raiders. Extract through a grate or onto your own Mat.
3. **Haul** into the Stash: unidentified items, condition, weight. Appraise what you can; the rest waits for a skilled slimo or a Bench.
4. **Decide**, per item: sell (eddies now), feed (growth and mutation now), craft (gear or product), keep (collection, set bonus, later value).
5. **Build and govern**: place structures on the Mat, set quotas and job priorities, sign or repeal laws, answer the foreman.
6. **Breed**: the feed plan steers the genome; organs come up; the Strainbook fills.
7. **Withstand**: the Attention your wealth generates arrives as a Patrol, a Sweep or a Cleansing; the city fights with what you built.
8. **Rise**: a Relay reaches the next stratum; new routes, new items, new factions, new white light.

Steps 5–7 keep running while you are inside step 2. The run clock and the city clock are the same clock; a Flood that starts during your run floods your city too.

**What a session leaves behind.** A player who stops after one evening owns: a Stash with items they chose to keep, a Runner who levelled or died, a Mat that reached somewhere new, a law on the Charter, two or three new Scrapdex and Strainbook entries, and a district they named. Every one of those is visible on the Stack view the next time they load.

**The two feelings the loop is tuned for.** Hoarding: the Stash grid fills with things of uncertain value, prices move, and the sell-or-keep call is never finished. Amassing: the city on the Stack view gets denser, brighter and more armoured, and the player can point at the part of it each run paid for.

**Perspective.** Side-on cross-section in every mode. Runs are played at Street zoom with direct control; the colony at Street, District and Stack zooms; Stash, Market, Strain Lab and Charter are full-screen panels drawn in the same pixel style over a dimmed view of the city.

## Expedition mode

A run is a real-time, side-on traversal of unlit sewer under direct control, with the Mat's edge as the only safe ground. Everything in it is built to make the player weigh weight. The run is one Rust simulation (`run::sim`) stepped at 20 Hz from a logged input word; Godot draws it and never decides anything.

**Traversal.** Slimos are ooze: they cling to and crawl along any surface, so a Runner moves in four directions through every passable tile of the cross-section (open floor and air, water, grates) and shafts need no ladders. Gravity is a rule for dropped items and bodies, not for slimos. Movement is continuous at a speed in tiles per second (sneak 1.5, walk 3.0, sprint 5.0 — a walk crosses the 20-tile view in about seven seconds; the cadence instrument set these down from 2.5 / 4.5 / 7.0, which cleared a route in under three minutes; overweight halves sprint; legs at 0 halve everything and forbid sprint). Collapse and deposit tiles are solid until dug.

**The Route Board** is the one screen between the Heart and a run, and it embarks in one click: three route cards across the top, the memory strip of the selected route under them, the crew on the left, the rig on the right, DEPART. A route is a stratum and a region (Sump East in S1, Culvert North in S2, Storm Drain in S3, Service West in S4, the Vault's Archive in S5, Undercroft Line in S6); the three cards are the strata from one below the city's to one above it — S1–S3 at the founding (Progression: The stack and the breach) and its seed is fixed by the save and the stratum, so re-running a route learns it. A card states what can be known before a run and nothing that cannot: length as chunks and shift minutes; danger as an index of the authored spawns — each burrow brings the mean pack of 2 drawn from the stratum's mix, each creature weighted crawler 1, burster 1.5, maw 3; each chalk-marked chunk brings two scouts (weight 3) at the stratum's patrol chance — shown as the number and banded by the index per chunk, LOW < 1.5, MEDIUM < 2.5, HIGH ≥ 2.5 (measured over 60 seeds per launch stratum: S1 ≈ 1.2 per chunk, S3 ≈ 2.1, S2 ≈ 2.4 because the culvert chunks carry the most chalk); the share of the route remembered from earlier runs; the lockers and crates seen on them (never the total); the water share of its tiles and the grates on it, and on a Flood shift how far the water rises and at what minute — "in overtime" on the shift before one (Phase D); our corpses by Runner and day; followers stranded there. The memory strip draws the selected route's remembered tiles at one pixel per tile (448 × 24 at most), the Mat edge in Ooze Glow, grates in Acid, known containers in Cyan, corpses in Hot Pink; unseen tiles are Void. The crew panel lists the Runner and the colony slimos who can follow (Phase C brings the records; the three named Phase B slimos stand in), two follower toggles. The rig panel packs from the Stash itself — the hand weapon, four quick slots, backpack items as a row of icons to click in, the weight against carry in Cyan and Hot Pink when over — and what is packed leaves the Stash at DEPART and comes home with the rig. Entry is at the Mat's farthest cord in that direction or at a grate you hold.

**Map.** Assembled per run from authored 32 × 24-tile chunks, 8–14 per run, seeded with the save so a route can be re-run and learned. Chunks are glyph grids in `data/chunks.toml` (19 shipped across S1–S3; 7 per stratum at launch) with exits in canonical windows — rows 11–13 on the west and east edges, columns 14–17 on the north and south — so any two facing exits join; unjoined exits compile to brick. A chunk's weight is both its pick weight and its cap per route (a vault once, a culvert up to three times); nothing is ever placed west of the entry column, which is where the Mat is. Validation rejects partial exit windows, open border cells, entries without an east exit, and any chunk whose exits do not all reach one another without digging; a route must reach ≥ 95 % of its passable tiles from the Mat edge without digging and 100 % with every collapse dug. Water level follows the city's Flood clock.

**Light** is a 0–15 integer field per tile, dark by default. Static sources: broken fixtures emit 6, street grates 9 (Night City leaking down), Mat tiles 2 + Lume gene × 6. Dynamic sources: the Runner's lume, scouts' lamps (10, white), muzzle flashes (10 for a tick). Light spreads by an 8-connected integer Dijkstra (orthogonal 2, diagonal 3, water ×3); solids take the arriving level and stop it so lit wall faces read. Three bands: dark ≤ 2, dim 3–6, lit ≥ 7.

**Lume and sight.** A Runner's lume = veins (Mod-Depth 1–5) + gear light (lantern 6, lamp pole 8, visor −1) + the Mat under their feet, −3 while dimming veins (held, 6 stamina/s), halved while Soaked, never below 1 for any slimo. A tile is visible when it is in the Runner's symmetric field of view and either lit ≥ dim or within the see-radius 2 + lume + Eye/2: dark rooms are seen as far as your own glow, a lit pool from across the chunk. Symmetry means anything that can see the Runner can be seen by a Runner looking that way.

**Exposure**, the light gem: max(light at your tile, lume), one number on the HUD. It sets the **notice range**, the distance at which anything can notice you: 2 + exposure tiles (dark ≈ 3, dim ≈ 7, lit ≈ 14), standing still −1, sprinting +2, digging +1.

**Noise** is a 0–15 footprint stamped over the tiles a sound reaches — open 2 per tile (diagonal 3), pipes 1 (a run carries footsteps far), brick 3 (through a one-tile wall), water 4 (halved reach), rubble and deposits 4, rock and concrete block — and it fades 2 per tick. Loudness: sneak step 2, walk 4, sprint 7, dig 8 per tick, container open 3, body pickup 4, dropped item 5, extraction channel 9, melee swing 3 + gear noise, gunshot 8 + gear noise; water adds 1, grates 2. Sewerats hear at 2, scouts at 4. The footprint is drawn as a brief cyan dither on the tiles it reached, pipes included, so nobody learns the rules through failure.

**Fog** has three states per tile — unseen, remembered, visible — drawn as a Void ordered dither at the art's own pixel grid (solid, heavy, by light). Remembered tiles persist per route in the save (`route_memory`), so a route is learned across runs and a death still pays in map. A creature outside visibility but inside hearing range is drawn as a one-pixel stir in its tile's dither for the tick it moved.

**Loot sources**: lockers and crates (tiered by chunk), scrap deposits (dig 6 s, 3 s with a Dig Claw, loud, into a pile on the tile), newcomer corpses (what a person carries down here; the body itself carried out for a recruit, filling half the rig and slowing you), Oathkeeper drops after kills, sewerat salvage, caches hidden behind a Sever (your Mat can grow into a sealed room on a later run if you leave a Bait), and the Vault chunks in S5 behind locks. Tables are data (`data/loot.toml`): each source names its picks, a NoDrop chance, a condition band, a category pool with weights and one tier row per stratum; a pick resolves NoDrop, category, tier, then an item uniformly from that category and tier. Every source reads its own stratum's row, and the rows are steep and gate the top the way level does: S1–S2 top out at Uncommon, Rare enters in S3, Blackline in the Storm Drains' crates (sealed corporate shipping) and S4's lockers and is an Undercroft crate's commonest tier, Relic only in caches; a crate is better than a locker by its picks, its condition and its row. Notable picks (Rare and up) run on a pseudo-random distribution — the chance ramps by a constant each miss and resets on a hit — so dry streaks and hot streaks both stay short; the constant is solved per row so the average still lands on the listed share. The first three container opens of a route's first run roll one row up. Contents roll from (route seed, run nonce, source index): where a locker is can be learned from the route card, what is in it cannot. Measured over 40 seeds a stratum: mean value per container roughly doubles a stratum to S4 and then climbs by half again a stratum against Blackline, the ceiling of what sells (S1 9 → S2 18 → S3 38 → S4 88 → S5 125 → S6 181 eddies; Pass 2's "each stratum up roughly doubles tier value"); past S4 the deep strata pay in Blackline's feed and the Vault's keys and caches more than in eddies.

**Tools buy certainty.** Crates are sealed corporate shipping: prying one with a Pry Bar is quiet (noise 3) and adds a pick, forcing it by hand is loud (6) with no bonus. Lockers are 30 % locked at tier 1, +5 % per tier; a Pry Bar opens one at noise 4, forcing by hand is noise 8 and half the contents lose 30 condition. The bar costs a 1 × 3 slot and 2 kg carried in before anyone knows if it will be needed.

**Opening and taking.** A source under the Runner shows its prompt; opening unrolls the manifest one item per 0.22 s, common first, the notable last on a longer beat, each line in its tier colour. Found items are unidentified: tier colour, category, size and weight show, never the name, and they sell blind at 40 % until appraised. Taking is into the rig by auto-place; what does not fit stays, so weight and grid are the limits, not a count. On extraction the rig is handed to the Stash and the intake is reported.

**Threats.** Every creature carries one awareness value 0–1: sight raises it by distance inside the Runner's notice range and only inside the creature's own field of view (the same symmetric shadowcast, so a creature sees the Runner only where the Runner could see it); sound at or above its hearing threshold sets a floor of 0.5 and a remembered position; it decays slowly and passes through every state on the way down. Four phases with visible tells: Calm (patrol or prowl), Suspicious ≥ 0.4 (walk to the last stimulus and look), Alert ≥ 0.8 (hunt the last known position, attack in reach), Search (Alert with no stimulus for 12 s: circle the last known position, then Calm). Ferals skip Suspicious.

Creatures path by a distance field over passable tiles to their target and move continuously at their speed; a kill leaves a salvage source on the tile (scout drops from the `scout_drop` table, sewerats from `rat_salvage`).

**Combat** is deliberate. Stamina 100: sprint 12/s, swing 15, dodge 20, dimming veins 6/s; regen 10/s after a second without spending; overweight halves regen and sprint. Melee (pipe, chrome fist, shiv, sword) has a wind-up (0.3 s for a pipe) then an active window on the tile ahead (sword reach 2); damage is the gear's; from behind the target's facing ×1.5. Dodge is 0.35 s of invulnerability across two tiles in the facing direction. Ranged scrap guns are a line of sight up to 12 tiles with small magazines, a reload, and the loudest sound a Runner makes. Salt from a bag lays a line sewerats will not cross. Ooze-time is Ivan's and only on Mat tiles: 0.2× for 6 s.

**Followers.** Up to two colony slimos come along and fight without being told: fists (4) or the melee they carry, one HP pool of 60 % of a Runner's total, the same Down and Dead timers, and creatures hunt them as readily as the Runner (a creature takes the nearest target it can see). Three orders: Follow (the default: stay within three tiles, path around walls), Hold (stand), Guard (hold the tile they stand on and strike anything within three tiles, then return). A follower backs off below 30 % HP — it stops seeking creatures and only strikes one on an adjacent tile — a Coward below 60 %, a Fearless never. A Runner who goes Down is picked up by the nearest Follow-order follower within six tiles, carried toward the nearest extraction tile at 0.6× walk speed for up to twenty tiles and set down there; a carrier standing on an extraction tile holds the channel for both. Items dropped by the Runner (the last thing taken, onto the tile as a pile) are followers' business only when ordered to guard them.

**The Runner's body** is four sections — head 20, torso 50, arms 30, legs 30, scaled by Body — and every attack type lands on a section by its own odds (a bite: legs 40 / torso 40 / arms 15 / head 5; a shot: torso 50 / arms 20 / legs 20 / head 10). Armour on the matching slot subtracts. A section at 0 transfers further damage to the torso; head or torso at 0 is Down; arms at 0 double dig and reload time and halve melee; legs at 0 halve speed, forbid sprint and double the extraction channel. Statuses: Bleeding (bites and blades; 1 HP per 4 s to the torso for 60 s or until antiseptic), Blinded (sight radius 1 for 4 s), Chrome Shock (Blackline weapons; locks one quick slot until a Bench clears it), Soaked (20 s after leaving water; lume halved), Fungal Fever (from a Burster; persists into the colony).

**Extraction.** Reach your own Mat's edge, or a grate you hold, or a neutral grate for a toll (eddies or an item). Channel 10 s; noise spikes (9 per tick); followers within 4 tiles extract, the rest are stranded at the extraction tile and recorded on the route card for the next run. A run ends in an outcome record: extracted or died, ticks, the rig or the corpse, followers extracted / stranded / dead, the Mat haul, regrown gear, kills, chunks seen, route memory — the colony reads this, the run never reaches into the colony.

**The shift clock.** A run is one shift: 8 min + 70 s per chunk beyond eight, so 8:00 for the shortest route and 15:00 for a fourteen-chunk one, counted down on the HUD and printed on the route card. Past zero the clock counts overtime in Hot Pink and the sewer answers every minute of it: another pack crawls out of a random burrow, and from S2 a patrol of two scouts rolls onto a chalk-marked chunk that has none; overtime runs into the next shift, so on the shift before a Flood shift that shift's water rises from overtime's first tick (Phase D: the run clock and the city clock are one clock). Nothing ends the run but the Runner; overtime only makes staying cost more.

**The Run HUD** is minimal and puts the senses on the Runner, where the eye is. Around the Runner, in world space: the exposure arc (left half-ring, Ooze Glow, filled by exposure / 15), the noise arc (right half-ring, Cyan, filled by the loudest sound at the Runner's tile in the last 0.3 s / 15), the notice ring (a dotted circle at the true notice range, so the distance at which anything can notice you is a shape on the floor), and during a channel the channel ring (filled by progress: Ooze Glow for extraction, Dust for digging). Top centre: the shift clock in Cyan mono, Acid under a minute, Hot Pink in overtime; under it the route strip, one square per chunk in placement order, lit when seen, Cyan for the one you are in. Bottom-left: the body as a silhouette of four sections (head, torso, two arms, two legs), each filled by its HP in Ooze Glow, Hot Pink at half, flashing at zero; the stamina ooze bar beside it; statuses as tags under it (Hot Pink for damage states, Acid for alerts); the Down countdown when down. Bottom-right: the hand weapon and the four quick slots as icons with their keys, the used slot flashing. Right edge: a chip per follower with name, HP bar and order letter, Hot Pink when down. Bottom centre: one prompt line, the key in Cyan and the verb in Chalk — OPEN, PRY, TAKE ALL, DIG, HOLD TO EXTRACT — and the controls hint for the first six seconds of a run only. The loot panel is grid to grid: on open it slides in at the right (the camera eases east so the Runner stays clear of it) with the source's items unrolling one per 0.22 s as tiles at their real size in their tier colour on the left and the rig's 8 × 5 grid with its placed items on the right; TAKE ALL moves what fits across and tints what does not Hot Pink. Event labels (damage, DOWN, EXTRACTED) flash above the body they belong to.

**The cadence budget and its log.** Pillar one is measured, not asserted. Every run writes a cadence log — one line per event with its clock time, in three classes: **landings** (a reward arrives: a container, deposit pile or body opened — a *pull*, the slot-machine moment; a notable revealed; items taken; a kill; a Mat-edge drop; extraction with its blind value), **losses** (damage to a section, bleed ticks, Down, Dead, a follower down or dead, overtime) and **decisions** (an open, an engagement — the first swing or shot in ten seconds, a dig, a salt line, an order, a drop, the extraction). The report at the foot of the log gives the run's length, counts per class, the median and 90th-percentile gap between landings and between pulls, notables, damage taken, blind value and value per minute. The budget the log is checked against, per route at launch: a pull every 60–120 s (median); at least one notable per run from S3 and one per three runs in S1–S2 (S1–S2 miss this by the loot rows' own rule — Rare enters at tier 3 — so the S1–S2 notable is the first-run generosity and the route's one crate, measured 0 for the bot: a lever for the loot pass, not the cadence one); a loss event at least every two minutes while the Runner is off the Mat (pressure never lapses); and a bot death rate of 10–30 %. The landing budget of research 02 — something lands every 10–30 s — is the whole game's: the run's landings are mostly its pulls, the rest come from the colony's cargo and crafts, so the landing gap is reported per run and judged on the combined run-and-colony log from Phase C — a route that kills a cautious bot more than a third of the time is a wall, one that never kills it is a corridor. The bot walks the lit route from source to source and never waits, reads a manifest or hesitates, so the design takes it as three times faster than a cautious player and checks it against the budget divided by three (pulls every 20–40 s, landings every 3–10 s, quiet under 40 s); that factor is an assumption until a recorded human run calibrates it in the Phase F playtest, and it is printed on every report so nobody mistakes it for a measurement. It also carries a design meaning: a route offers about three times the pulls a shift consumes, so a run is a choice of what to leave and a route stays worth re-running.

Measured at PAC-028, twenty bot runs per stratum: at the Phase A speeds (walk 4.5) and dig 4 s the bot cleared a route in 2:25–3:20 with a pull every 7–8 s, half of them deposits (16 per route in S1); after walk 3.0 / dig 6 s and deposits thinned to one per chunk (two in collapse and hazard chunks) a run is 3:37–4:58 (×3 = 11–15 min, the shift), a pull every 11–12 s against the bot band of 20–40 s, deaths 20–30 %, longest quiet 42–70 s against 40 s. The instrument also caught the loot roll walking a map in process order, so two runs of one seed disagreed; the roll is sorted and a seed replays bit-exact again. The standing finding is source density: routes offer 10–12 containers and 10–12 deposits over 11 chunks (~2 pulls per chunk) where the budget wants about one; the chunk library's growth to forty is where it closes (target ≤ 1 container and ≤ 1 deposit per chunk on average, entry and nest chunks above), and the player factor is calibrated in Phase F. **The city's log** (PAC-044) is the same three classes on the city's clock (a tick is half a second, and the run's clock and the city's are one): landings — a sale paid as it lands, a feed landing at a Pit, a product home, a structure built or upgraded, a district formed, a law reaching a district, an election; losses — a death, a strike, a slimo ready to defect, cargo spilled, a structure losing its signal; decisions — a ghost placed, a cut marked, a quota or a priority set, an order, a law signed. `rose city` plays the whole game headless for twenty shifts: each shift a bot run on the first route card of S1 with the eligible slimo of best Scav as the Runner, its haul into the Stash, then the city's shift in `game`, with a steward standing in for the player — the Heart's Pit kept at 2 nutrient (Uncommon ceiling), every Curio and Tech sold at the first grate while the Mat reaches it, a Relay at a time toward the first grate whenever the cord to it is lost (on the anchor joined to the Heart nearest the grate along the ground the Mat can grow on, the next when it stands, at most eight standing), a build plan placed one ghost at a time at the nearest anchor to the Heart in Pass 2's first-session order (Hab-Pod; Lantern Post; Stockpile Rack with eight cells; Hab-Pod; Bunkhouse; Charter Board; Salt Works) that waits after the first Pod while the cord is being won back, every petition answered by placing what it asks — and the city ticking through each run at the bot's pace: the bot plays a route three times faster than a player, so the city takes three of its ticks for every ten of the run's and a shift does to the city what a player's shift does (125 Mat steps); the Runner packs the bot's kit from the Stash where the Stash has it, and the bot does not go back for its dead. It asserts that the city replays bit-exact, that every shift's cargo lands within the next shift, that the build plan stands, that every shift ends with the cord to the first grate standing or a Relay able to go out to win it back, that nobody starves and the Heart's core keeps its signal, and it judges the merged run-and-city landing gap against the budget (the bot's band, the run's factor printed), writing `docs/telemetry/city.md`. The steward's orders are this design's, a stand-in for a player, not a strategy the game recommends. **What it found** (PAC-044): two ways cargo locked the Mat for good (cargo waiting on the Heart's tile for a grate it could not reach; feeds and overflow meeting head-on on one cord — both closed in Cargo), a floor of 0.2 under the meal that a fed Mat never reaches (Homes, meals), a full grid's overflow out of the economy (now the Heart's floor, Stash), and a roster that only shrinks (Recruitment: the flush); with those closed the city replayed bit-exact, its cargo landed, the plan stood and the landing gap merged was in the bot's band, and it failed on the founding: the scale stratum's Mat (grown 500 ticks on the Heart and seven Relays) receded once its founding feed stopped, the cord to the first grate went at the fifth shift with or without a steward, and slimos starved from the eighteenth. **What PAC-045 found**: the instrument gave the city a third of each shift (it ticked the city at 2 Hz on the bot's clock, so the Mat lived about 35 of a shift's 125 steps); two processes could disagree on a replay (the Pits drawn in a map's order, a rig's weight summed in a map's order — both now in a fixed order); and the call between a smaller Mat, a different starting economy and a steward who feeds the cord came out as all three, in measured parts. A smaller Mat: the founding is Pass 2's first session to the grate (The founding). The economy: Pass 1 tuned the Mat's upkeep and decay on a feed about fifty times what a Runner's haul brings the Heart, and over sixteen seeds with the cord founded and the steward steering, it was lost within 2–8 shifts on every seed at half Pass 1's rates, on nine at a quarter (won back on five), and held on fourteen at an eighth (won back on the other two) — the Mat's metabolism is an eighth (The Mat's metabolism). The steward (these three at half Pass 1's rates): feeding the far end of the cord from a Pit there held it on 1 seed of 8 (the pump splits the Heart's food over the whole Mat and the far tissue starves first); steering by supply alone from a founding fifteen tiles out, the haul painted ahead of the Mat, reached the grate within twenty shifts on 1 seed of 8 at one haul a shift and on 6 at three; a Relay a shift at the frontier reached it on 8 of 8 in 5–8 shifts — so the steward steers with Relays, and a Relay stands on any signalled Cord or Node (it needed a Node, and on most seeds none stood near the frontier). `rose city` passes on its seed (11) and holds or wins back the cord on all of seeds 11–26. On five of them (13, 14, 15, 23, 26) the first route card kills 4–8 of twenty Runners, more than the flush brings, and the city thins to 0–3 and its plan stops: Phase B's death band is a mean (10–30 %) and these cards run 20–40 %, and the steward never changes card — choosing routes is the Route Board's and the player's. The Deposits carry the founded Mat: left alone for twenty shifts with them, it holds the cord on 13 of 16 seeds unfed and 14 fed; with them taken off the map, 0 of 16 at one haul a shift and 7 at two. A haul is about two covered Deposits' income, so at these rates the feed-or-sell dilemma does not reach the cord; the haul's place in the Mat's economy against Pass 2's "over-sold Mat starves" is the economy pass's (PAC-061). Phase C closes here.

The log is written to the save folder (`user://telemetry/`), the player can read it, and `rose cadence` plays seeded runs with the bot (`run::bot`: open what it sees, take what fits, fight what is adjacent at over 40 % HP and back off under it, extract when the rig is nearly full, the shift is under 90 s, or the body is under 30 %) and writes the aggregate against the budget to `docs/telemetry/cadence.md`. Measured figures live in that file and the ones the budget hangs on are repeated here when a stage changes them.

**Death and recovery.** Down lasts 60 s untreated, then Dead. A dead Runner leaves a corpse at the tile of death holding the whole rig, identified; the route card marks it, and on the next run of that route it is a loot source like any other. Raiders roll once per colony day since the death at 10 % + 5 % per stratum; a raided corpse is stripped and the body stays. Several corpses may lie on one route. Items dropped on Mat tiles (the entry window and the two cord tiles beside it) come home as a separate Mat haul whether the Runner lives or dies. Gear flagged **Spored** at the Bench (a craft using Spore Sac tissue) regrows at the Heart on the Runner's death, once, and is removed from the corpse so nobody recovers it twice. Everything else is gone.

**What a run pays**: loot, newcomers, route memory, Runner XP, chunk intel (Corpo contacts buy maps), and salvage of the things that tried to kill you.

## Items

Every item has the same eleven fields, so every item can be sold, fed, crafted or kept and the UI never special-cases.

| Field | Values | What it drives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category | Chrome, Part, Organic, Tech, Gear, Contraband, Key, Curio, Relic | Which verbs make sense; which vendor wants it |
| Tier | Common, Uncommon, Rare, Blackline, Relic | Base value, drop weight, mutation drift when fed |
| Size | w × h grid cells (1×1 to 4×3) | Rig and Stash space |
| Weight | 0.1–12 kg | Stamina drain, noise, extraction speed |
| Condition | 0–100 | Sell price multiplier, craft yield, gear stats; decays with use and water |
| Identified | no / partial / yes | Unknown items show a silhouette and a tier guess; appraisal reveals name, value and feed vector |
| Found-in-run | tag | Contract eligibility, Corpo premium; stays until sold |
| Feed value | nutrient + 10-gene bias vector | What it does to the strain in a Pit |
| Craft tags | e.g. Conductor, Blade, Optic, Enzyme, Housing | Which Bench recipes accept it |
| Set | optional set id | Three of a set in the Stash gives a passive (see Curios) |
| Provenance | stratum, chunk, day | Codex entry, trophy text |

**Categories at launch**

- **Chrome (38 types)**: junk implants, street cyberware, corpo hardware, Blackline components. Feed value scales with tier; Chrome Affinity bias on anything with a Conductor tag.
- **Parts (24)**: pipe, wire spool, lens, servo, pump, filter cloth, cell, board, bearing. The craft backbone; low sell value, high craft value, which is the first sell-or-keep lesson.
- **Organics (14)**: food paste, rat jerky, clean water, mould culture, antiseptic, splint, stim, Downer dose. Needs and medicine.
- **Tech (16)**: data shards (sell to Corpo or read for route intel), blueprints (unlock research), access cards, maps.
- **Gear (31)**: melee (6), ranged (7), armour (6), tools (lantern, dig claw, pry bar, salt bag, lure, repair kit), implants (6, installed at the Bench, permanent, raise a stat, add a weakness).
- **Contraband (6)**: Downer, cold light, filtered water, ooze enzyme, Oathkeeper vestment cloth, Night City comms gear. Highest margins, highest Attention.
- **Keys (8)**: grate keys per zone, Vault cards, a lamp-carrier badge that lets one Runner pass a Patrol once.
- **Curios (22)**: the collection layer: pre-vaccine street signage, a corpo nameplate, a child's ooze toy, a chaplain's rosary of lunar stone. Sets of three give passives (Street set: +1 scav yield; Arasaka set: locked lockers open by hand; Reliquary set: Attention decays +1 per shift; the seven under Progression: Collections).
- **Relics (5)**: lunar reliquary fragments. Feed value 1.0 with Plasticity bias; unsellable; one of them is the key to the Hive ending.

**Identification.** An unidentified item shows its silhouette, size and weight only. Appraisal happens by a slimo with Appraise skill ≥ the item's tier (instant, in the Stash), at a Bench with an Optic part (one shift), or by selling blind to a Gang fence at 40% of value. The identify moment is a reveal with a stinger per tier.

**Condition.** Water and use lower it; a Repair Kit and a Bench raise it. Below 20, gear jams and chrome loses half its feed value. A dropped item on a wet tile loses 5 per shift. Organics spoil: 2 a shift wherever they lie but a Cold Store (this design's number; it is what a Cold Store is for).

**Price.** base × condition/100 × the stall's faction's demand for the category (0.6–1.6, moving with the surface's clock) × reputation × the stall's haggling × glut (down 5 % per unit of the category sold at that market per shift, the price recovering 20 points a shift) × found-in-run premium (1.25 at Corpo) (Economy). The Stash shows three numbers on every item: sell now, feed now (cells of growth at current intake), craft into (best recipe). Keep is the fourth verb and it has no number, which is the point.

## Stash, inventory and the Bench

**Rig.** Each Runner carries a backpack grid (6×4 to 10×6 by backpack tier; 8×5 until backpacks are gear), a chest rig with 4–8 quick slots (items of one or two cells, usable in combat; the 1 × 3 pry bar rides in the backpack and is found there), two hands, a head, a body and a feet slot, and four implant sockets. Weight above the Runner's carry stat (18 kg at Body 5) halves sprint and doubles noise. The rig is the run's inventory: loot is taken into it by auto-place and it is handed to the Stash whole on extraction.

**Stash.** The city's inventory lives at the Heart as a grid that grows with the Heart's tier, which is the city's (Hamlet, Quarter, City, Empire): 12×10, 16×14, 20×18, 24×22. Its other rooms are the stockpiles (Colony mode, Stockpiles) and the Heart's floor, where what the grid has no room for lies (a haul's overflow, a product home to a full grid; it tries the grid again at every shift end and a stockpile at 1–2 takes it): the Stash screen pages between the Heart's grid, its floor and each stockpile, and every verb works on an item in the room it lies in — a Sell or Feed from a stockpile rides from that stockpile's Rack, Craft and the Appraisal Table take it where it is, the quotas pull from every room. Containers (crates, lockers, a Vault) are items that hold items: they sort, filter and protect (a Vault hides Blackline from Attention). Sort by category, tier, value, weight, condition; filter to unidentified; a search box. Hovering an item shows the three numbers and its codex line.

**The four verbs** are on every item's card, in this order, as buttons: Sell, Feed, Craft, Keep.

- **Sell** queues the item to a market of choice. A market is where the Mat has reached a grate (the colony's market sites); with none reached the verb is closed and the card says why. At shift end the queue leaves the Heart as cargo (Colony mode, Cargo) and each item is paid when it lands at its market, at the price of the faction whose street the grate is (Economy: base × condition/100 × demand × reputation × haggling × glut × blind): glut is −5 % per unit of the item's category already sold at that market this shift (counted as they land), floor 25 %, the price recovering 20 points a shift; an unidentified item sells blind at 40 %; a stall that buys pays at least 1 e; an item the faction will not buy (a Relic, Blackline outside the Corpo remnants' tier 3) is refused at the card. The quote counts what is queued and riding to that market ahead of it. A queued item sits in the queue panel and can be pulled back into the grid until the shift ends. Eddies land in the ledger on the save.
- **Feed** takes Chrome and Relics — the items with a feed value; the Mat does not eat the rest — to a Pit of choice (the Heart or a built Pit). At shift end it leaves the Heart as cargo, and when it lands the Pit's stock rises by the item's nutrient (halved below condition 20) and the genome drifts by the item's bias vector × the tier's drift (Common 0.01, Uncommon 0.03, Rare 0.07, Blackline 0.15, Relic 0.40), each gene clamped to 0–1. The card's "feed now" is cells of growth: nutrient / what a sprout costs its parent cell (grow cost 0.04 + tip starter 0.12 = 0.16), about six cells per unit of nutrient. A Pit's quota feeds it without a click (Colony mode, Cargo).
- **Craft** opens the Bench with the item in the first slot that takes one of its craft tags (recipes land with the Bench, PAC-027).
- **Keep** pins it: a pinned item is outside every bulk action and counts toward sets and the Scrapdex.

**Bulk actions.** The filter bar (category, tier, condition below N, unidentified) selects a view of the grid; the bulk bar applies Sell or Feed to every unpinned item in that view, showing the count and the eddies or nutrient it totals before the click — sell all unpinned Common Chrome, feed all Chrome below condition 20. Sort by category, tier, value, weight or condition. The grid draws every item as a tile of its real size in its tier colour with the icon centred, 32 px cells, scrolling when the Stash outgrows the screen; nothing is ever stretched.

**Shift end.** The run clock and the city clock are one clock, so the shift ends when a run ends: the queue and the quotas' pulls leave the Heart as cargo, and the Stash shows the shift report on the way back from the run — what landed during the shift (sold, earned, fed, drifted, spilled) and what has just left.

**Bench.** A Tweaker Bench has recipe tiers 1–4 (research) and a queue. A recipe takes items by craft tag rather than by name (Blade + Housing + Conductor = scrap sword, whose stats come from the parts' tiers and condition), so there are 60 recipes and thousands of outcomes. Crafted gear carries the Runner's name and the parts' provenance. Products: gear, implants, Downer, cold-light cells, filter cartridges, salt bags, Spored tags, repairs.

- **Recipes** live in `data/recipes.toml`: sixty over four tiers (15 / 17 / 16 / 12). Tier 1 is open at launch; tiers 2–4 open when a blueprint of that tier is researched (Phase E). A recipe is two to four slots; each slot names a craft tag and the stat it drives (dmg, armour, noise, light, mag, condition, or nothing — a binder). Any item carrying the tag fills the slot, whatever its category: a Rare Gold Trace is a conductor the same as a Common Wire, and that is the difference between the two swords.
- **Stats from parts.** A part's tier moves the stat its slot drives, measured against the recipe's own tier: ±15 % per tier above or below for dmg, armour, light and mag (clamped to 0.7–1.6 × base); −1 noise per tier above, +1 per tier below (floor 0). Product condition is the mean of the parts' condition with the housing slot counted twice. The outcome preview shows every number against the base before the click (SWORD dmg 13 → 15, noise 2 → 1).
- **Provenance.** A crafted instance records who made it (the Bench until Phase C names a slimo with Tweak), the day, and every part by name and tier; it keeps its own stats, and the run reads the instance, not the def. Identified, from the city.
- **Products** are an item id (gear, implants, parts, organics, contraband) with a batch count for consumables (salt bags and cells come in threes), or one of three jobs on a piece of gear already in the Stash: repair (+40 condition, capped at 100), identify (an Optic part on an unidentified item, one shift), Spored (Spore Tissue or a Spore Cap on gear: it regrows at the Heart once).
- **Queue.** A job takes its tier in shifts (1, 1, 2, 3) and finishes at shift end into the Stash, because the shift is the one clock. Parts leave the Stash when the job is queued; cancelling returns them. A full Stash leaves the product at the Bench until there is room.

**Appraise** is a Bench job and a slimo skill; a D1 trader with Appraise 4 identifies a Blackline item instantly, which makes that slimo worth protecting.

**Hoarding pressure.** The Stash is finite, prices drift, the Mat is hungry and the Bench wants parts. The design never forces a sale; it lets a full Stash with three unidentified Rare items in it sit on the screen when the next run is loading.

## Runners and slimos

Every slimo is a full character record; a Runner is a slimo you have taken on a run. The record is what the player loses when one dies, so it has to be worth losing.

**Stats (1–10):** Body (HP, carry), Nerve (stamina, dodge, lume control), Hands (melee, dig speed, craft quality), Eye (ranged, appraisal, fog radius), Mat-sense (signal reading, Shepherd bonus, cord riding; set by Mod-Depth), Voice (trade, foreman ability, Mood aura).

**Skills (0–10, XP by doing):** Scav, Fight, Tweak, Shepherd, Trade, Appraise, Medic, Guard. A skill at 5 unlocks its mid-tier job; at 8 its mastery perk (Scav 8: containers show their tier through fog; Tweak 8: one free part per craft; Shepherd 8: tips within 6 tiles extend at double rate).

**Mod-Depth** sets the body, the role ceiling and where they can go. D1 Skin-deep passes for human (veins 1; topside with a coat on; staffs a Seep Market; no Mat bonuses; Mat-sense 1). D2 Limbed (veins 2; anywhere underground; the scavenger body: Body +2, Hands +1, Scav +1; Mat-sense 3). D3 Core-modded (veins 3; anywhere underground; builds at 2× on Node tiles and senses σ; Hands +2, Nerve +1, Tweak +2; Mat-sense 5). D4 Deep (veins 5; Mat-bound — σ below 0.2 hurts them — and rides cords at 3× walk; Nerve +2, Body −2, Voice −1, Shepherd +3; Mat-sense 8; cannot run, since a run leaves the Mat). D5 Dissolved (a flesh pile; every stat 1; cannot act; can be fed to a Pit as a tier-2 unit at −5 city Mood and a line from Ivan). **Pasts** (Gang, Nomad, Corpo, Badge, Civ) give a stat lean and a field bonus: Gang Nerve +1, Body +1, Fight 2; Nomad Eye +1, Nerve +1, Scav 2, Shepherd 1; Corpo Eye +1, Voice +1, Appraise 2, Tweak 1; Badge Body +1, Voice +1, Guard 2, Fight 1; Civ Hands +1, Trade 1, Medic 1. A faction's reputation moves with its Past's slimos (Phase D).

**Creation.** A record rolls from the save's seed: depth by the campaign's weights at launch (D1 30 %, D2 40 %, D3 20 %, D4 8 %, D5 2 %; chapter 5 shifts the weight to the deep), a Past (Civ 30, Gang 25, Nomad 20, Corpo 15, Badge 10), every stat at 4 then the depth's and the Past's leans then three random points moved one at a time, clamped 1–10 (Mat-sense is the depth's); the field bonuses as skill levels; two traits from the forty with no excluded pair; a name from `data/names.toml` and, one time in four, an epithet; needs full and Mood at 60.

**Skills and XP.** Skill levels cost 10 + 5 × level XP each (325 XP from 0 to 10) and XP comes from doing, in the run outcome and the shift report: Scav 2 per pull and 1 per item taken; Fight 3 per kill and 1 per hit survived; Guard 2 per follower strike; Tweak 4 per craft finished, 2 per shift on Research and 1 per shift on Build; Trade 2 per sale resolved; Appraise 3 per item identified; Medic 3 per treatment; Shepherd 1 per shift on Tend. Teacher in the district multiplies XP by 1.25; a trait's own multiplier stacks. A skill unlocks its mid-tier job (Research needs Tweak 2, the Appraisal Table Appraise 3, the clinic Medic 5); at 8 the mastery perk (Scav 8: containers show their tier through fog; Fight 8: wind-up −0.1 s; Tweak 8: one free part per craft; Shepherd 8: tips within 6 tiles extend at double rate; Trade 8: glut recovers in one shift at that slimo's market; Appraise 8: identifies Relics; Medic 8: fractures heal in one shift; Guard 8: strikes two creatures a swing).

**Traits**, two per slimo, drawn at creation from forty in `data/traits.toml`, each a typed effect the crates read, never flavour alone: stat leans, a need rate (Fed, Lit, Dry, Signal, Rest multipliers), a Mood lean, a loved and a hated job (working the loved job +10 Mood target, the hated −15 — the genre's passion, research 03), a skill XP multiplier, rig rows, and flags the run and colony check. The forty: Hoarder (rig +2 rows, Nerve −1, loves Scav), Night Eyes (see radius +1, Lit need ×0.5), Loud (noise +1, Voice +1), Steady Hands (Hands +1, melee noise −1, Tweak XP ×1.5), Claustrophobic (Mood −10 in one-wide tunnels, hates Scav), Devout (Mood +10 near white light, defects at 25 not 20), Chromehead (feeds Chrome to themselves: Body +1 per five fed, Mood −5), Teacher (district XP ×1.25, loves Tweak), Glutton (Fed ×1.5, Mood +5 above Fed 80), Cold Blood (no panic in white light, Nerve +1), Pack Rat (Curio picks ×1.5, loves Scav), Iron Gut (Fed ×0.7), Light Sleeper (Rest regen ×0.7, loves Guard), Heavy Sleeper (Rest regen ×1.4, hates Guard), Brawler (Fight XP ×1.5, melee +1, hates Tend), Dead Shot (ranged +2, Eye +1), Quick (sprint +0.5, stamina regen ×1.2), Lumbering (walk −0.3, Body +1, carry +4), Mule (carry +6, hates Research), Frail (Body −2, Fed ×0.8, Appraise XP ×1.3), Webbed (wades and swims at walk speed, Dry ×0.5), Dry Skin (Dry ×1.5, hates Tend), Mat-born (Signal ×0.5, Mat-sense +1, loves Tend), Topsider (Signal ×1.5, Trade XP ×1.5, loves Appraise), Gloomy (Mood −8), Sunny (Mood +8, Voice +1), Fence (sells at +10 %, loves Haul), Tinkerer (Tweak XP ×1.5, loves Tweak), Healer's Hands (Medic XP ×1.5, treats at 2×), Coward (followers back off under 60 % HP, Nerve −1, Rest regen ×1.2), Fearless (never backs off, Nerve +1), Scrounger (deposit piles +1 item, Hands −1, loves Scav), Sharp Nose (notices creatures a tile farther, Eye +1 in the dark), Strong Back (carry +8, Body +1, Hands −1), Soft Voice (Voice −2, Appraise +1), Orator (Voice +2, election weight ×2, hates Scav), Hermit (Mood +10 alone in a Pod, −10 in a Bunkhouse, hates Guard), Pack Animal (Mood +10 in a Bunkhouse, bonds in two runs), Old Chrome (Body −1, Tweak +1, one more implant socket), Sewer Lungs (immune to Fungal Fever, Dry ×1.2). Pairs that cannot coexist are excluded in the data (Glutton/Iron Gut, Light/Heavy Sleeper, Coward/Fearless, Gloomy/Sunny, Hermit/Pack Animal, Quick/Lumbering, Mat-born/Topsider, Loud/Soft Voice). Traits are the reason two D2 Gang scavengers play differently.

**Needs** are six 0–100 gauges that move once per shift from what the slimo did and where it was, each rate multiplied by its traits: Fed −15 (a meal from the Mat on signalled tissue with nutrient, or two eddies of paste, fills it); Lit −10 in the dark and +20 in light at or above dim (D4 has no Lit need; a D1 loses 15); Dry −25 wet and +15 dry; Signal −20 off the Mat (D4 −40) and +25 on signalled Mat; Rest −12 working, −6 idle, +30 asleep in a Pod during Dark, +15 asleep anywhere else. Thresholds change behaviour: Rest below 20 halves speed; Fed at 0 costs a point of Body per shift; Signal at 0 on a D4 is a wound; Mood below 20 refuses jobs. **Mood** is a target the gauge moves toward at +4 / −3 per shift (research 03): the target starts at 60, every need below 40 takes 10 and below 20 another 10, every need above 80 gives 5, the loved job +10 and the hated −15, each injury −5, a death this shift −the dead slimo's Voice (×2 for a bonded partner), the trait's Mood lean, and from Phase C's structures the city-tier expectation and the Charter's laws. The unrest lines at 30 / 20 / 10 — strike, defection, petition — are read off the gauge by the Charter (PAC-042); the record only says which line it stands below.

**Jobs by utility.** Nine jobs (Haul, Build, Scav, Tweak, Tend, Guard, Cook, Appraise, Research), each read through one skill (Haul and Scav through Scav; Build, Tweak and Research through Tweak; Tend through Shepherd; Guard through Guard; Cook through Medic; Appraise through Appraise), and a 1–4 priority per slimo per job, 0 for never. A run is the Scav job for its Runner and the Guard job for its followers: the shift's Rest cost and the loved and hated leans read it. A slimo's utility for a job is None when the depth forbids it (D5 anything; D4 anything off the Mat — Scav, Guard at a gate, Cook; D1 Tend), when the skill gate is unmet, when Mood is below 20, or when Rest is below 20 and the job is not rest; otherwise priority × (0.6 + skill / 10) × the trait's affinity (loved 1.4, hated 0.5) × the need pressure (Fed below 30 doubles Cook, Rest below 40 halves everything but Tend). The colony hands each open job to the highest utility in reach; a district's priorities replace a slimo's when the foreman's are higher (PAC-041), and at equal utility the slimo's own priority decides, so a 4 the player set is not lost to a foreman's 4 on a slot listed earlier (PAC-081: `rose campaign`'s researchers lost their Bench to the foreman's Build that way).

**The record in a run.** The Route Board hands the run a `RunnerSetup` read off the record, and the run's numbers bend to it by one rule per stat. Body scales the four sections and the stamina pool's twin, carry: sections × (0.8 + 0.04 × Body) (Body 5 is the table's numbers, 10 is ×1.2, 1 is ×0.84) and a carry limit of 18 kg + 1 kg per point of Body above 5 (−1 below) + the traits' carry. Nerve scales stamina the same way: 100 × (0.8 + 0.04 × Nerve), so Nerve 10 is 120 stamina and Nerve 1 is 84; a bonded partner within 6 tiles is +1 Nerve, +4 stamina. Hands moves melee damage by (Hands − 5) / 2 rounded toward zero (Hands 10 +2, Hands 1 −2, never below 1) and dig time by × (1.25 − 0.05 × Hands) (Hands 5 the table's 6 s, Hands 10 4.5 s, Hands 1 7.2 s). Eye is already the see radius (2 + lume + Eye / 2) and moves ranged damage as Hands moves melee. Veins are the depth's and set lume. The backpack is 8 × (5 + the traits' rows), 3–8 rows. The trait flags the run reads: Night Eyes see radius +1; Loud every Runner sound +1; Steady Hands melee noise −1; Brawler melee +1; Dead Shot ranged +2; Quick sprint +0.5 tiles/s and stamina regen ×1.2; Lumbering walk −0.3; Webbed wades and swims at walk speed; Scrounger a dug pile rolls one more item; Sharp Nose Eye +1 on a tile darker than dim; Sewer Lungs no Fungal Fever; Coward and Fearless the follower back-off line above. The rest of the forty are the colony's (PAC-041/042) or the Bench's. Dodge and dimming read Nerve in Phase D's combat pass; until then they are the table's.

**Injuries** persist: Bleeding (treat with antiseptic), Fracture (splint; −Hands or −legs for 3 shifts), Chrome Shock (locks an implant socket until a Bench clears it), Soaked, Blinded, Fungal Fever (from a Burster; Ferality gain doubles until a Lung treats it). A Cook at a Lung Housing — the Cook job reads the Medic skill, and the clinic's slot asks Medic 5 — treats injuries over shifts; without it they heal at a quarter rate.

**Gear slots**: head, body, feet, two hands, chest rig, backpack, four implants. Implants are permanent and visible on the sprite.

**Death.** HP 0 is Down; 60 s untreated is Dead. Dead Runners go on the memorial at the Heart with their record; their corpse and gear stay in the run chunk. A district whose foreman dies holds a one-shift wake (no jobs); Mood falls by the slimo's Voice.

**Recruitment.** Newcomers at drains (free, random record), rescued bodies on runs (random record, loyalty bonus), defectors from raider groups you spared (Gang Past, Fight-heavy), hires through faction contacts at reputation +2 (a tier-4 service, 150 e: the faction's Past with its Past skills raised two — Economy). Population is the growth cap of everything else, which is why bodies are worth carrying out. **The flush** (Pass 1's numbers, Pass 2's first-session beat; PAC-044): a batch of 1–6 bodies for each stratum of the grate (Progression: Newcomers by stratum) comes down a storm-drain grate every 9–15 shifts (Pass 1's every three to five days; a day is three shifts), the first at the end of the city's first shift (Pass 2: the first batch drops at the grate at minute four), and each grate the Mat reaches keeps its own clock (Progression: City tiers; the census makes it 6–10 shifts and four shifts' wait); each is a record rolled by Creation, D5 included (Pass 2: "the other is D5"). A newcomer is the city's once the Mat reaches a grate and a bed stands free for them; until then they wait at the grate — a yellow alert — and one not sheltered within two shifts goes feral in the drain and is gone (Pass 1), a loss on the city's log. So beds are recruitment: a Hab-Pod placed is a newcomer kept. `rose city` found the need: with a Runner's death every five runs (the bot's 20 %) and no flush, six founders are two by the twentieth shift and too few to build, tend or eat. The rescued bodies, defectors and hires arrive with their systems (Phase D).

**Bonds.** Two slimos who complete three runs together (two for a Pack Animal) gain a bond: +1 Nerve when within 6 tiles of each other, and the survivor of a bonded pair takes a Mood hit twice the normal size. No romance system; bonds are a run mechanic. The roster, its records, its priorities and its bonds live in the save (v6) and the Route Board's crew is the roster's eligible slimos: D1–D3, up, not already out.

## Colony mode

The city has every system a player of the genre expects to find, and each one is bent by the fact that the ground is alive.

**Feature baseline and the ROSE version of each**

**What the genre does not have**

- The ground grows, starves, mutates, speciates and eats; Pits, Bait, Salt and Severing are the steering tools; organs grow themselves at gene thresholds.
- Districts self-govern: a hub Node with three structures elects a foreman; the foreman posts jobs and cargo demand and petitions you.
- The Charter: laws propagate as a signal wave; eight laws at launch (Rationing, Open Grates, Conscription, Quarantine Protocol, Dissolution Rite, Tithe, Curfew, Foreman's Cut) with costs in Mood and Authority (the table under Colony mode).
- Nerve logic: up to four when/then rules per Relay over signal, nutrient, Attention, water, traffic, white light; actions sever, gate, quota flip, alarm, dim, dump.
- Attention: wealth and light you show at a grate bring the white light down; three stages, each with salvage.
- The handoff: a strain with Plasticity and Conductance above 0.7 proposes its own reroutes.

**The Mat's growth** is Pass 1's cellular automaton (its tab keeps the spec and the numbers) with five readings this design made when the stack had to be climbed (PAC-081): a frontier that stalled for want of food in a corridor never grew again, and the city stopped forty tiles under the next grate however it was fed. Pass 1's own principle — "the frontier speed is set by supply, not by a timer" — decides each. **Reawakening**: a strand's end (a Cord with one hyphal neighbour, the Tip that stalled) that the network has fed to the extension reserve is a Tip again, at the lateral sprout's rate. **A lateral sprout** enters ground touching up to two hyphae, the extension's own limit (Pass 1 states none for the sprout; one left a Mat that filled both sides of a shaft's foot unable to enter it). **The Heart pump** pushes surplus to the hyphal neighbours farther from the Heart along the tissue, and to lower signal only where the tissue does not lead to the Heart: Pass 1 wrote "farther from the Heart (lower σ)", and a Relay's own peak of σ turned the Heart's food back at every Relay, so a Relay carried the signal up a shaft and starved what grew past it; a Relay is a source of signal, not of food. **The breach** pours the Mat in: each tile a slab opens beside tissue is a Tip, as a Relay raised sprouts its neighbours (Pass 2: "the camera following the first tip up the shaft"). Measured on seed 11 with a Relay and a Pit fed 1.1 a shift at the frontier each time it stalled: the Storm Drains' slab broke in shift 27 where before the city stopped at row 232 for a hundred shifts; the twenty-shift founding holds the first grate on all sixteen seeds 11–26 (before: two seeds never won it back). **The Heart and the Relays seed again**: a Heart or a Relay the tissue round it has died from, fed to the extension reserve, puts its empty ground out as Tips as it did at its seeding or raising (Pass 1: "the Heart never starves", and a Relay is a Node that never starves; PAC-081: a Sweep's bleach killed every tile round a Heart, and a Node with no strand's end never grows, so the city stood at one tile for sixty shifts — and a Relay left alone in a bleached shaft stood there for a hundred with the climb cut above and below it).

**Building.** A structure stands on the Mat by its anchor — the top-left tile of its footprint — which must be Cord or Node carrying signal at or above the structure's need (0.3 for most, 0.5 for the organ housings — the Lume Farm on a Lume Bulb, the Lung Housing on a Filter Lung — and the powered Reliquary Vault and Scrap Turret); the Mat is filaments and nodes, not floors, so the rest of the footprint rests on walkable sewer floor (open, pipe, brick, grate, deposit; never rock, concrete, water or a collapse) or on more Mat. The ones marked *Node* need a Node under the anchor, a Seep Market a grate within two tiles, a Relay Shrine a Relay. Footprints never overlap. A footprint that is not square can be turned (R) before it is placed; the anchor stays its top-left tile. Placing makes a ghost; the first Build shift pays the cost out of the Stash — Parts are any Parts-category items (the cheapest first), Chrome any Chrome-category items, eddies from the ledger — and a ghost the Stash cannot pay for waits and says what it lacks. Building is the Build job: every slimo on Build adds their Hands in build points per shift (a Scrap Forge in the district doubles them); a structure is up when its points are met. Build work — mending first (below), then ghosts, upgrades and marked cuts — is taken first asked, first done, two builders to a ghost or an upgrade and one to a cut, and the crew of a ghost the Stash cannot pay for goes on to the next work in that order, so a short Stash never idles a builder. Three visual tiers (the Heart four), upgraded in place: tier 2 costs twice tier 1 and needs the Quarter, tier 3 three times and needs the City; the Heart's tier is the city's. HP is 100 × tier (the Heart 200 × tier, a Sphincter Gate 400 × tier); the Flood, flamers and crawlers take it (Pressure), and at 0 the structure is ruined. **Mending** is Build work and comes before the rest of it (this design's: what stands is worth more than what is asked): a built structure under its HP takes two builders from the next shift's plan, each mending 2 × Hands HP a shift — 10 at Hands 5, a Scrap Forge in the district doubling it as it doubles build points — and it costs no Parts (research 20: RimWorld and 0 A.D. both repair for work alone), only the shifts the builders would have spent on the ghosts. A dark structure does no work of its own; building on it, an upgrade or a mending, goes on.

**Zones** are painted cells on signalled Mat (σ ≥ 0.3, Cord or Node) or on walkable floor touching it: a **stockpile** (around a Rack or Cold Store, with a filter and a priority; below), a **hab** zone (beds inside it are assigned before any other), a **quarantine** zone (a slimo with Fungal Fever sleeps and works inside it), a **kill** zone (Guards strike and turrets fire at anything inside without an order). A cell belongs to one zone.

**Stockpiles** (research 17). A stockpile is a zone painted against a built Rack or Cold Store; it holds one item a cell, up to the store's cells (8 / 12 / 16 by tier). Its filter is categories, a tier floor and a condition floor; its priority is 1–4 on a job's scale, the higher wanted first, and the Heart's grid stands between 2 and 3 (research 17: RimWorld's storage priority, where haulers move what a better store accepts). At shift end, after the queue and the quotas' pulls have left, the Stash settles itself: a stockpile at 4 or 3 draws what it accepts out of the Heart's grid and out of any stockpile below it; one at 2 or 1 takes only what the grid cannot hold — a haul's overflow, a product home to a full grid — 2 before 1; what moves rides the Mat as cargo to the store's anchor. A new Rack's priority is 2 (it catches overflow and moves nothing) and a new Cold Store's 3 with an Organic filter (it draws the food out of the Heart). Cargo lands on the stockpile's Mat cells at once; its cells off the Mat take an item only when a **hauler** carries it there — each Haul slot at the store shelves as many items as the hauler's Body a shift, 1 Scav XP an item (Haul's skill) — and an item that finds no free Mat cell waits on the store's own footprint (2 cells for a Rack, 4 for a Cold Store). So without haulers a stockpile holds its Mat cells and its footprint; haulers make the whole painted zone count. A stockpile whose anchor is lost spills nothing: its items wait on the zone until a store stands again.

**The steering tools** (the tool bar; Pass 1's costs, which Pass 2 keeps as the Mycelium branch's first tier). **Pit** arms the Build tool with the Pit; **Relay** arms it with the Relay (6 Chrome and 16 build points, Pass 1's six Street Chrome and two tweaker-shifts at the eight points a Pit prices one). **Bait** smears the cheapest Common Chrome in the Stash onto the 3 × 3 around the click (The city's clock). **Salt** lays one tile the Mat will not enter; a bag of road salt from the Stash lays eight (Pass 1), the open bag's remainder carries to the next tile. **Sever** marks a tile of Mat to be cut: the cut is Build work, 8 points (Pass 1's one tweaker-shift), taken at shift end by a slimo on Build like a ghost, and a marked tile can be unmarked until then. **Build** opens the Build menu; **Zone** paints and erases zones; **Overlay** cycles the overlays.

**Orders.** Left-click with no tool selects what is under the cursor (a slimo, a structure, a tile of Mat) and opens its card; hovering shows the same card small. Right-click is the order: with a slimo selected, right-click a structure with a slot and the slimo works it the next shift — over its priorities and its schedule's Any, never over sleep in Rest, a strike, a wake or a run; the order is spent with the shift. With nothing selected, right-click cancels the ghost or the cut under the cursor; with a tool armed, it puts the tool down. A structure's card holds its upgrade (UPGRADE places the next tier's ghost over it), a Pit's or Still's quota, a stockpile's filter and priority, and its slots with who works them.

**Alerts** are the crates' to raise and the HUD's to show, on the right edge, newest on top, one new line a second at most, each jumping the camera to its tile when clicked and clearing when its cause does. Red is loss in progress: a district on strike, slimos ready to defect, a structure whose anchor has lost its signal, a slimo starving, a death, cargo spilled. Yellow is a decision: a petition, a ghost the Stash cannot pay for, a quota whose consumer the Mat no longer reaches, a jam that has stood a minute (120 ticks), items on the Heart's floor for want of room in the grid. White is the Oathkeepers (Phase D).

**Overlays** draw one number a tile over the Street and District views: nutrient, signal, light (the Heart's 4 tiles, a Lantern Post's 3, a Lume Farm's 4 / 6 / 8), zones by kind, and traffic; noise and danger arrive with the threats (Phase D). The camera follows a selected slimo (F), and bookmarks hold nine views (Ctrl + 1–9 sets, 1–9 returns); the clock pauses on Space and runs at 1×, 2× and 4× (2, 4 and 8 ticks a second) on + and −.

**Homes, meals, light, water, sleep.** Each slimo takes a bed — in a hab zone first, then the nearest free bed to the Heart; a Hermit takes a Pod over a Bunkhouse and a Pack Animal the reverse — and sleeps on the Mat when there is none (Rest +15, never +30). A shift is Dim, Dark or Flood in turn (shift *s* of the day is *s* mod 3), and the Pod's +30 is Dark's. **Schedules**: each slimo's row gives the three shifts Work, Rest or Any — Work takes a slot, Rest sleeps, Any takes a slot while Rest is 40 or more and sleeps below it; the default row is Any / Any / Any, so a city works until it is tired and sleeps when it is, at any hour; Curfew sends it home in Dark. A slimo whose shift was a run worked it and takes no slot. A slimo eats from the Mat when the signalled tissue about its home (the cell and the eight round it) holds the meal, and the meal takes 0.05 nutrient from it, the richest tile first (Pass 1: population costs nutrient; `rose city` measured the tissue round a fed Heart at 0.13–0.19, so the 0.2 floor this replaces put a fed city on paste, and found several sleepers drawing one anchor tile to nothing while the tissue round it held plenty — PAC-045), and only what each tile holds past its extension reserve (the growth threshold, the sprout's cost and the new tip's starter: what it would grow with), so the city eats the Mat's surplus and never its growth or its life (PAC-081: four hundred slimos drew their meals down past the starvation line and the Mat round every home, the Heart's own, starved to nothing; held at that line, they still ate every nutrient the trunk carried up the shafts, and the frontier stood for fifty shifts on tissue that lived and could not grow); when it does not, two eddies of paste from the ledger feed it; when the ledger is dry it goes hungry. A home is lit when it is within a Lume Farm's, a Lantern Post's or the Heart's light; dry when its cell is not water or a Lung stands within 6; signalled at σ ≥ 0.3. These are the `ShiftContext` the record's needs read.

**Districts and foremen** (research 14). A district forms where a Node has three built structures within 12 cords (Pass 2: the distance walked along Cord and Node from the hub to the nearest tile of the structure's footprint on the Mat); the Node is its hub, the structures within 12 cords that no nearer hub claims are its own, the members are the slimos whose bed is in it, else whose job is. The city tier caps districts at 1 / 3 / 8 / 20 (the Heart's own is the first). A district holds an election the shift it forms and every ten shifts after, and at once when the foreman dies or leaves: each member's weight is Voice + Mood / 20 (Orator × 2; a slimo on a run or stranded cannot stand), the highest wins, ties to the longer-serving. The foreman's job priorities stand over a member's where they are higher, the foreman posts the district's cargo demand (Cargo, below), and the foreman petitions. A district whose foreman dies holds a one-shift wake (no jobs) and its members' Mood target falls by the dead slimo's Voice.

**Petitions** are the foreman's mandates, and a district petitions only through a Charter Board (the Heart's district through the Heart's own board). When a district's members average under 30 in a need for two shifts running the foreman asks for the structure that answers it — Fed a Pit, Lit a Lume Farm (a Lantern Post will do), Dry a Lung Housing, Rest a Bunkhouse (a Pod will do), Signal a Relay Shrine — with five shifts to build it in the district. One petition stands at a time; met, the members' Mood target gains 10 for three shifts; missed, it loses 10 until the structure stands; refused, it closes and counts toward the district's dissolution (below). The petition is a line on the Colony HUD's alert queue, on the Charter and on the Slimo card of the foreman.

**The Charter** (Pass 2, carried in full; research 15). Ivan signs laws. A law is a signal pattern that leaves the Heart and takes effect in each district as the wave reaches it — the wave walks Cord and Node at 16 + 32 × Conductance tiles a shift, so a district within that many cords of the Heart takes a law the shift it is signed and a far one a shift or more later; a slimo in no district takes it when the wave reaches its bed (the Heart, if it has none). Laws cost Authority to sign and Mood to keep. A repeal is free, refunds nothing and travels the same wave; signing again costs again. There is no signing cooldown: Authority paces the Charter.

The Authority column is this design's (Pass 2 gives none): a first law costs one, so the first district formed pays for the first law, inside Pass 2's 20–40 minute beat. A law that is not yet live can be signed; its Mood and Authority are real at once and its effect arrives with its system. **Authority** is earned by deeds — a district formed +1, a Patrol repelled +1, a Sweep +2, a Cleansing +3, a contract chain finished +1, a chapter beat +2 (Progression: Authority) — and spent on laws and Parley tiers; it never comes from scrap. Ivan's four Authority states (his sprite, his portrait, his bark pools) follow the Authority earned over the run, never the balance — 0–2, 3–6, 7–12, 13 and over — so signing does not shrink him. **The vote**: when a law is signed, each district with a Charter Board votes through its foreman, against when the law costs its members Mood and for when it does not, and the Charter keeps every law with its shift and its vote (Pass 2's board at the Heart).

**Dissolution.** A D5 (Mod-Depth 5, cannot act) can be dissolved into a Pit from its Slimo card: 0.30 nutrient of Street grade (Pass 2; today's Uncommon) at the Heart's Pit and its name on the Memorial. Without the Rite it is the per-event Mood hit: every slimo's target −10 the shift it happens (the number is this design's); with the Rite, no hit and the standing −3.

**Unrest** (Pass 2), read off a district's Mood — the average of its members':
- below 30 for two shifts: **strike**, the members take no jobs until the average is back at 30;
- below 20: **defection** — each shift each awake member below 20 leaves for good with 1 in 4 to a feral strain if one borders the district, or to the Oathkeepers if a Patrol is in its zone; both arrive in Phase D, and until then the district shows it is ready to defect; nobody asleep in a Pod during Dark defects (research 03);
- below 10: **the foreman petitions** to repeal the law that costs the district the most Mood, or, with no law to blame, for the structure its worst need lacks; answered (the repeal or the structure), the Mood target gains 10 for three shifts; **refusing twice** dissolves the district — its hub is removed, its foreman stands down, its structures lose signal priority (the cargo serves them after every other consumer) and the Node cannot hub again for ten shifts.

A single slimo with Mood below 20 refuses jobs whatever the district does (the needs-below-20 override). Unrest is how the city tells the player a law or a quota is wrong, in the only language a self-governing system has.

**The city's clock** (research 16). The city ticks at 2 Hz in every mode: cargo moves a tile a tick, a slimo a tile every two. The Mat steps once every 12 ticks, 125 steps in a 12.5-minute shift — the shift Pass 1 tuned the CA's rates on (0.004 nutrient a tick is 0.5 a shift), now that a shift is a run of 8–15 minutes and not Pass 2's 60 seconds; at 2 Hz the Mat lived eleven of its shifts in one run. The Mat eats only what the city feeds it: its Pits (a feed, a quota's pull, a dissolved D5), the Deposits it covers (+0.004 a step each, Pass 1) and Bait — the tool bar's Bait smears the cheapest Common Chrome in the Stash onto the 3 × 3 around the click, its nutrient spread over the nine, so the Mat grows toward it on nothing the Stash did not give (this design's; Pass 1's Bait Smear painted 0.3 onto five tiles for one Junk unit, eight times its cost). The other steering tools and their costs are under The steering tools. **The founding** (PAC-045) is Pass 2's first session to 5:30 — the Heart and a Pit of six Junk Chrome; "a grate is visible 40 tiles east, the Mat is 15 tiles out, second Pit, baited east"; "the Mat reaches the grate" — which in this game's clock falls inside the first shift's run, so the founding plays it rather than the player: the Heart grows from alone on Pass 1's feed of 0.6 a step, half of it painted on the ground the Mat touches nearest the first grate (the grate nearest the Heart, the distance walked over the ground the Mat can grow on, so a shaft that dead-ends under rock is not nearer for being close across), until tissue joined to the Heart is within two tiles of the grate (a market's reach); then the Pit holds six Junk Chrome. The Heart is born with every gene at 0.5 (Pass 1), the strain the player breeds from; on seeds 11–26 the founding takes 69–576 steps and leaves 107–290 cells (until PAC-080 it founded a Vigor and Conductance 0.8 strain, never written here, which by Pass 2's lines was a Runner from the first minute: a class is earned, so the founding is Pass 1's). Pass 2's text gives the beats; fitting them before the first run is this design's reading, and the 500-step scale stratum it replaces stays in `rose scale`. **The Mat's metabolism** is an eighth of Pass 1's: upkeep 0.000075 a hyphal cell a step (Thrift's multiplier on it) and decay of 0.0125 % of every cell's nutrient a step, where Pass 1 tuned 0.0006 and 0.1 % on a feed of 0.6 a step, about fifty times what a Runner's haul brings the Heart (1.1 a shift, measured in `rose city`). At an eighth, one haul a shift and the Deposits the Mat covers hold the founded Mat and its cord to the first grate; at a quarter the cord breaks on most seeds faster than the founders' Relays (two shifts each) win it back; at half it breaks within eight shifts fed or not (What PAC-045 found, under The city's log). The scale proof's three thousand slimos and synthetic market hauls (Phase A) stay in `rose scale` as the load test of the arrays; the game's city has the roster's slimos and the Stash's cargo, nothing else.

**Cargo** (Pass 2's Automation pillar, carried in full; research 16). The Mat is the conveyor: Scrap, water, Downer and cold light move along cords as discrete packets, one tile per tick, routed by demand. The player never carries anything; they decide where demand is and watch the network answer it.

- **What rides.** The Stash is at the Heart, so a haul arrives at the Heart (the run hands its rig to the Stash) and every trip starts or ends there. At shift end the Stash's queue leaves as cargo — a Feed order to its Pit, a Sell order to its market — and so do the quotas' pulls; what a Still or a Salt Works makes rides home to the Stash. One item is one packet, and its effect lands with it: nutrient and drift into the Pit; eddies into the ledger at that market's glut at that moment; a Part into the Still's store; a product into the Stash grid (onto the Heart's floor while the grid is full). What is on the cords is in the save and rides again from the Heart on load. Brine is the city's, not cargo: a Still uses the brine the city's Lungs made that shift (v3.9). The Bench and the Appraisal Table work the Stash where it is.

## Economy

Two currencies and one tax. Eddies buy from the surface; Authority buys laws and parley tiers and is earned only by deeds; Attention is the tax every eddy and every lume pays at the grate. The economy is the `economy` crate on the city's clock, its factions, stock, contracts and surface clock data in `data/factions.toml` (PAC-061, research 19).

**The streets above the grates.** Every grate on a stratum's top row opens onto a street one faction works, and a market at that grate trades with that faction alone (Pass 1: "which grate you reach decides who you meet"). The first grate — the founding's — is always the Nomads' (food paste and salt, the Sump's lifeline); each of the other three trading factions works at least one more grate of the stratum, and the rest roll by the stratum's weights — Nomads 3, Gangs 3, Badges 2, Corpo 1 in the Sump, the Badges heavier along the Service Tunnels and the Corpo remnants in the Vault (Pass 1's strata), rolled from the save's seed at the founding (this design's). The grate's gauge carries the faction's name once the Mat reaches it. **Open Grates** lets every market trade with every faction at +1: an item goes to whichever pays most.

**Vendors.** Five factions in data, the Oathkeepers trading only at the envoy's grate after an accepted accord (chapter 3: Narrative and Ivan). Each has a buy list — a demand per category and a few items of its own — and stock in four tiers.

Kinds (the Full Design's four): **deliver** n of a category (and tier) at condition ≥ c to the faction's market — the items ride there as a sale does and count as they land instead of being paid; **clear** — raiders on runs (n killed, offered once raiders walk the Board's routes) or a Bloom (no Bloom tissue left in the city, offered while one stands); **recover** — a named corpse's tag or a named item, put in a body at the far end of a route on the Route Board (the Board's route of a stratum seeded among those it offers, on the floor farthest from the Mat edge along the Runner's ways, where raiders land on a route with no grate), brought home and delivered; the body shows on the route's card under its name and raiders roll on it each day as on any of ours, so a slow recovery can find it emptied; **host** — a named newcomer of the faction's Past joins the waiting list and must be in the city, alive, five shift ends later. A link has three shifts (four for clear and recover). Link k asks and pays 1.55^(k−1) times the first (this design's: a five-link chain pays 14.5 times its first link, Pass 2's "triple the sum of five singles"); its reward is eddies and favour; the last link adds +1 reputation, the next stock tier outright and the faction's unique item (a Nomad backpack, a Gang monowire, a Corpo appraiser implant, a Badge shock lance, at condition 90). A miss breaks the chain and costs one reputation.

**The Downer chain** is the mid-game engine: Lung brine + ooze enzyme (a Still with a slimo on Cook) + a Part with an Enzyme tag → Downer dose. Sells to every faction, highest to Corpo (Nomads 1.0, Badges 1.2, Gangs 1.3, Corpo 1.5); every Still adds 8 Attention per shift to its zone; the Oathkeepers' flamers go for the Stills first in a Cleansing (Pass 1: "the Oathkeepers' first target"), then the Pits, then the Heart. The decision the chain forces: how many Stills before the gauge beats the income.

**The haul against the ground** (measured in PAC-045, set in PAC-061). At the Mat's metabolism the Deposits it covers (+0.004 a step each, Pass 1: half a nutrient a shift) carry the founded Mat: a Runner's haul brings the Heart about 1.1 a shift, two covered Deposits' worth, and left unfed the cord to the first grate holds twenty shifts on 13 seeds of 16. So the feed does not decide whether the city lives. Measured in PAC-061 (`rose economy` on seeds 11–26, thirty shifts each, the steward trading against the steward feeding, below): selling the haul's Chrome instead of feeding it earns about two fifths more — 11,889 e against 8,519 over the sixteen cities, more on fifteen of them (re-measured in PAC-062 with the steward's services) — and buys as much reach: the nearer grate fell in four cities either way, the rest reaching none in thirty shifts. What the Heart's feed buys is the cord: the cities that sold their Chrome went without a market for 50 shifts in all, those that fed it 24; on seed 21, whose Deposits did not carry the founded Mat, the selling city lost its market for 25 of the thirty shifts and ended with nothing, while the fed one lost it for 7 and reached the nearer grate. So the haul's Chrome is the early game's choice between eddies now and insurance on the cord; the next grate, and with it the next faction, waits on the steering (Relays and Bait), which this steward spends only on the first grate.

**Attention** is the tax, kept per grate zone and spent by the Oathkeepers (Colony mode, Pressure): Lume near a grate, eddies traded at it, Blackline sold there and Blackline and Relics outside Vaults, Stills, Oathkeepers killed; 2 a shift decays; Corpo intel reads it exactly. Thresholds 30 / 60 / 90 for Patrol / Sweep / Cleansing; each stage is also salvage.

**Sinks** so eddies stay scarce: food paste (2 per slimo per shift when the Mat cannot feed them), the vendors' stock and services, hires, Lantern oil, structures' eddy costs, research scrap, Stash expansions (Phase E). Early game the player is eddy-poor and chrome-rich; mid-game eddy-rich and Attention-poor; late game the question is what to do with wealth that cannot be shown.

**The proof** (Phase D): `rose economy --seed 11 --shifts 30` plays the city thirty shifts with the steward trading — selling its surplus, the haul's Chrome past a Relay's six too, at whichever market in reach pays most, five of a category a stall a shift; taking, while it holds no chain with a faction, the three-link deliver contracts it can fill now and the three-link host contracts while a bed is free, and keeping a host home from the runs; buying protection when a Patrol is due at a Gang grate, intel when a reached zone stands within 15 of the Sweep line, the Badges' timings when one stands within 10 of any line, the Nomads' convoy every shift it sells at their stall once their tier 3 is open, and a hire when a bed stands free and the eddies cover it twice — and asserts that every faction's demand for every category stayed inside its band (buy-list value ± 0.3, inside 0.6–1.6) every day, reporting the realised price of every sale against its base, the chains, the reputation and the eddies; then it plays the seed again with the steward feeding as `rose city`'s does (the Heart keeping 2.0 nutrient on the haul's Common and Uncommon Chrome, the founding's standing order) and reports when each reaches the nearer grate and how long each went without a market.

**The Strainbook** is 38 cards in `data/strain.toml`: the 12 organs, the 5 classes, the Relic state (three classes at once) and 20 notable mutations — a reflection off 1.0 for each of the ten genes (Pass 2: "a gene reflecting off 1.0"), a reflection off 0, a merge shock (Pass 2: a graft taking feral tissue back), a Relic fed, two classes held, a class lost, the first Bloom, a single feed moving a gene 0.1 or more, a gene at 0.9 or more, a Mat of 2,000 hyphal cells, and the handoff's first proposal.

**The handoff** (Pass 2, bred, never bought): on a strain with Plasticity and Conductance above 0.7, a jam that has stood a minute (120 ticks) makes the Mat propose a bypass — the shortest path of growable ground, at most 16 tiles, from the cord before the jam to the cord after it that keeps off the jammed tiles — drawn dashed in Ooze Glow in every view, a yellow alert that jumps to it, CONFIRM and SEVER on its tile's card; confirmed, the Mat lays it as tips a tile a Mat step, paid from the Heart's nutrient at the growth cost; severed, it is dropped. One stands at a time, and the first is the Strainbook's last card.

**Route loops** (Route automation; Pass 2: "a route that returned twice can be assigned to a crew on a loop"). A route the Runners have come home from twice can be put on a loop with one slimo of the eligible crew: every shift its run is played headless by the run bot on that route with that slimo's record and a kit from the Stash, and lands at the shift's end as any run does — the haul, the XP, the injuries, the death. Each repeat gives every container and pile a further 10 % chance of nothing (Pass 2's decay), 5 % with a Nomad or a D2 on the loop, to 90 %; a run by hand on the route resets it. The loop slimo is away that shift as a Runner is; a loop whose slimo dies on it ends. The Route Board's card carries it: LOOP: n OF 2 RUNS HOME, then LOOP IT WITH the Runner picked, then the slimo and its repeats with END.

**Nerve logic** (Pass 2, carried in full). A Relay Shrine holds four rules, *when* [reading] [< or >] [value] *then* [action], evaluated every 20 ticks (the cargo field's beat). The readings are at its Relay: signal, nutrient (the 3 × 3's mean), Attention (its grate zone's), water (its zone's Flood rise), traffic (packets on cords within 6), white light (tiles to the nearest lamp, 99 with none within 30), strain (feral tiles within 12). The actions: *sever here* (the outward cord beside the Relay is marked to cut, once), *shut gates* and *open gates* (Sphincter Gates and Sphincters within 12, while the rule holds), *flip quota* (Pits within 12 pull nothing while it holds), *alarm* (the district's D2s are militia while it holds, as Conscription's), *dim* (Lume Bulbs and Lume Farms within 12 give no light and no Attention while it holds), *dump* (one Blackline from a Reliquary Vault within 12 rides to the nearest Pit as a feed, once a shift). Pass 2's "rules propagate as a signal pattern" is the reading: a rule reads only what its Relay's signal reaches. A Hive strain with Chrome Affinity over 0.6 lets a Relay Shrine stand on any Node, its rules read there (Pass 2: "cords themselves carry the rule without a Relay"). On the Shrine's card each word of a rule cycles on a click — the reading, < or >, the value through its steps, the action — X clears it and + RULE adds one; HOLDS marks a rule holding now.

**Authority** (Ivan's; Pass 2: "earned by chapter beats, repelled attacks and district formation"). A district formed +1; a Patrol repelled +1, a Sweep +2, a Cleansing +3 — repelled when the stage leaves past its break line (half its bodies fallen) with the Heart standing; a contract chain finished at its last link +1; a chapter beat +2 (each chapter's close: Narrative and Ivan). Spent on laws (the Charter) and Parley tiers (Ivan's, PAC-081). Pass 2's pace of one law each 2–3 hours is a deed every 10–15 shifts.

**City tiers** (Pass 2). The population's tier — Hamlet below the Quarter's 100, Quarter, City at 500, Empire at 2,000 — caps districts at 1 / 3 / 8 / 20, sets the Heart's tier and the structures' upgrades (tier 2 the Quarter, tier 3 the City), and lets a grate hold a second Seep Market from the Quarter (one before). Each of the four counts — 20, 100, 500, 2,000 — is a title card the first time it is reached (the Empire's is the 2,000th slimo waking). Each grate the Mat reaches has its own flush clock (Pass 1: the flesh piles arrive "at storm-drain grates"), so a city's growth is its reach; the batch grows with the grate's stratum (below).

**The stack and the breach** (Pass 2's Stack view, "the whole 192 × 288 stack"; Pass 1's six strata; PAC-081). The city's world is the stack: six strata of 192 × 48 tiles in one Mat of 192 × 288 — the Sump at the bottom (rows 240–287), the Undercroft at the top (rows 0–47) — generated whole from the save's seed at the founding, each stratum by its own weights in `data/strata.toml` after Pass 1's table: the Sump's black water and silt, the Culverts' brick, the Storm Drains' wide water, the Service Tunnels' conduits, the Vault's concrete rooms and the Undercroft's metro tunnel. A stratum's top row is its ceiling: rock, with a grate over each of its eight shafts (every 23 columns from 10), and beside each grate the shaft runs on into the stratum above, sealed where it crosses by a **slab** — rock the Mat cannot enter and nothing walks. A grate opens onto its street whatever lies above it (Economy: the streets), so every stratum's grates trade, flush and send the Oathkeepers down as the Sump's do. **A breach** opens a slab: a built Relay within 4 tiles of it at a shift's end, or the stratum's grate key spent on it from the slab's card — the Sump Grate Key opens the Sump's ceiling, the Culvert Grate Key the Culverts', the Drain Grate Key the Storm Drains', the Service Grate Key the Service Tunnels', a Vault Card the Vault's (Pass 2: strata "by Relay reach or key"; the keys are the run's). The stratum above is **reached** when the Mat joined to the Heart first holds a tile of it (a Bloom's tissue that touches the city's is not the city's — PAC-081: a Bloom grown under a slab stood in for the climb, which raised no Relay there): Ivan's line, the alert, and the camera following the first tip up the shaft (Pass 2). The city's stratum is the highest reached, and it sets three things: the stages that can come (Pressure: Sweeps from S4, Cleansings from S6); the Route Board's three cards, the strata from one below the city's to one above it (S1–S3 at the founding, S4–S6 once the Vault is reached; the lowest card is the city's stratum − 1 held to 1–4); and the Attention baseline — every reached zone gains a point a shift for each stratum the city stands above the Sump (Pass 2: "Attention baseline rises per stratum"; the number is this design's). A stratum not yet reached draws as Void with its name in Dust on every view, and the minimap shows the strata from the city's down. The Heart stays in the Sump (this design's: Pass 1's chapter 6 "may relocate" it, but a Heart moved orphans every cord the founding grew; the Undercroft gets the council instead). Floods rise the Sump's water as before — Pass 1's "lowest occupied stratum".

**Newcomers by stratum** (the campaign's pace; Pass 2's tiers). A flush's batch is 1–6 bodies for each stratum of its grate — the Sump 1–6, the Storm Drains 3–18, the Undercroft 6–36 (this design's: the higher the grate, the nearer the street the bodies fall from) — at each reached grate's own clock of 9–15 shifts (6–10 with the census), and from chapter 6 the occupation doubles every batch (Pass 2: the occupation drives Night City's own down). A city's growth is its reach upward.

**Collections**, in the save and across runs (Pass 2: "the Strainbook persists across runs"; New Game+ keeps the Scrapdex; the profile carries them between runs — Meta, below):

- **The Scrapdex**: every item but the Organics, 150 of the 164 — an entry is seen when an item of it is identified; the Codex shows the rest as silhouettes.
- **The Strainbook**: the 38 cards above.
- **Curio sets** (seven; three of a set in the Stash, pinned or not): Street +1 scav yield (every pile dug and container opened rolls one item more); Arasaka, a locked locker opens by hand as an unlocked one, no forcing and no damage (it read "locks open 2 s faster", and a run's locks have no timer: forcing is their cost); Reliquary Attention decays 1 more a shift at every grate; Feral, sewerats notice a Runner a tile later; Road, +2 reputation favour a sale at a Nomad, Badge or Gang stall; Heart, the Heart lights 2 tiles farther and its Pit holds half again; Leak, Seep Market sales +5 %. - **The Memorial**: the dead with their record and the shift they died.
- **Trophies**: twelve — the first kill of each of the three sewerats (crawler, burster, maw), the four Oathkeeper roles the city meets (scout, sprayer, lamp carrier, flamer) and a raider, on a run or in the city, and each of the four hazards (a Flood, a Bloom, a Sweep, a Cleansing) lived through with the Heart standing.

**Scav yield** is what a run's sources roll: the Street set's +1 is an item more from every pile and container; Conscription's cost is a 1 in 5 chance of nothing from each while the Heart's zone is under a Patrol or worse when the run leaves.

**Difficulty curve.** Threat scales with stratum and Attention, never with time alone, so a careful player can stay small and safe in S2 for as long as they like and feel the ceiling of it: S2 loot tops out at Uncommon, and the Oathkeepers only arrive when invited by wealth.

**Meta** (Pass 2). A finished campaign — any ending, Scattered included — exports a **strain code**: `ROSE-` then the genome as ten two-digit hex values (0–1 across 00–FF), the classes held as two hex digits (bit k the k-th class of `data/strain.toml`), the seed as eight and a checksum byte — 32 digits in eight groups of four, typed back with case, spaces and dashes forgiven and a digit off refused (this design's: a code a person can read out; the lineage stays in the profile beside it). A run's seed is 0 to 4,294,967,295 so the code carries all of it. **New Game+** takes a code, typed or the last run's: the Heart is born with the code's genome, every law the last run signed is unlocked on the Charter (signable without its tech, Authority still paid), the Scrapdex is kept and the Stash is empty (Pass 2). The **profile** holds what outlives a run — the Strainbook, the Scrapdex, the endings reached with their warchests and codes, the laws unlocked, the Daily bests and the Endless ranks — in `user://profile`, and the Codex reads it (an entry seen or earned in any run counts). The run's Strainbook and Scrapdex reach it at every shift's end, an ending (or an Endless city's burning) when its screen is shown; a profile that does not read is set aside as `profile.bad`, never written over. Endless Sump and the Daily Seed: Narrative and Ivan.

**Endings** read off the dominant genes and the alliance at the Great Cleansing — Garden, Hive, World-Eater, Accord and Scattered, rules and scores under Narrative and Ivan.

## Art bible

One style, named **Sewer Noir 32**, applied to every pixel on screen including placeholders. The rules below are the whole style; anything not covered is decided by them.

**Grid and scales.** World base 640 × 360, integer-scaled (3× at 1080p, 6× at 4K). Tiles 32 × 32. Slimos 32 × 48; Ivan and lamp carriers 48 × 64; sewerats 32 × 32; items 32 × 32 icons in a 32 px inventory grid; UI on a 4 px rhythm with 8 px margins; chapter panels and codex art at 1280 × 720 (exactly 2× the world, so panel pixels are world pixels doubled).

**Line.** 1 px selective outline in Void on every sprite, item icon and UI frame; outline breaks where a form turns into shadow (selective, never a full silhouette trace). Tiles carry no outline; their edges are read from value contrast. This is the one rule that makes the world photographed and the people drawn.

**Value and colour.** Three-step ramps, hue-shifted: shadows pull toward Drain Blue, lights pull toward the nearest emitter's hue. Two-tone ordered dither at 50% and 25% for gradients; three-tone gradients never. Clusters of pixels follow form (no single-pixel noise, no banding bands). Saturated colours are emitters only.

**Per-module plans**

- *Tiles*: 47-tile blob autotile per material (concrete, brick, pipe, water, grate, collapse), wet highlight 1 px in the nearest emitter's hue on every horizontal, parallax planes behind (cavern wall, distant tunnel) and in front (pipes, cables).
- *Mat*: cords 8–12 px wide at 32 px tiles with an Ooze Dark core and an Ooze Lit edge, pulse in Ooze Glow outward from the Heart; translucency faked with a dither of the tile beneath; organs and class decorations as node overlays.
- *Slimos*: five depth bases × skin × inclusion overlay × vein overlay × gear layers; D4 bodies use the Ooze ramp with Chalk inclusions. Walk 8 frames, idle 4, work 6, carry 6, down 1, dig 6, swing 4, aim 2.
- *Ivan*: the only 48 × 64 slimo; Ooze ramp at full saturation, Hot Pink rim light, four Authority states.
- *Oathkeepers*: 48 × 64, Vestment White with Reliquary Gold trim, bulky Y2K hardware, lamps on poles; their light is the only pure white and it casts hard shadows from cords.
- *Items*: 32 × 32 icons on a shared 24 × 24 inner silhouette grid, tier shown by a 1 px corner mark in the tier colour, condition by rust overlay bands.
- *UI*: 9-slice frames whose background ends exactly at the frame line; two fonts (a 5 × 7 pixel monospace for numbers and terminal text, a 7 px-cap condensed grotesk for labels and narration); labels slam in with a two-frame glitch; Cyan numbers, Acid Yellow alerts, Hot Pink confirmations, Vestment White for anything Oathkeeper.
- *VFX*: sparks, ooze drips, bleach foam, flame, bullet trails, dig dust, extraction channel ring; all 2-tone, all on the emitter rule.
- *Panels*: 1280 × 720 pixel stills, hard ink, high contrast, text boxes in the grotesk.

**PixelLab pipeline.** Three style anchors are made first (one brick culvert tile panel, one D2 slimo, one Rare chrome item), curated by hand, and every later asset is generated with `generate_image_bitforge` against them at high style strength; `rotate` for the side-on flip, `animate_with_skeleton` for the eight-frame cycles, `inpaint` for gear layers over a base body. Generation sizes 32 / 64 / 128 only. Every import passes the palette-lock validator (quantise to SEEPAGE v2, reject if more than 2% of pixels moved more than one ramp step), the size check and the outline check (1 px Void on the alpha edge). Tiles are generated as 64 × 64 panels and cut.

**Placeholders.** One language, generated by `tools/placeholder` from the manifest: exact size, a flat fill in the slot's ramp (tiles Noir, Mat Ooze, slimos Flesh, items Industrial, UI Sump), a 1 px dashed Void border, the slot's three-letter tag in the pixel monospace, and a silhouette hint (capsule for a body, rounded rect for an item, hatch for a tile). A placeholder obeys the grid and the outline rule, so a screen of placeholders already reads as one style; it is obviously unfinished because of the dashes and the tag, never because it breaks the grid.

## Narrative and Ivan

Ivan Ooze is the voice of the game and the one character the player cannot replace. He is purple ooze with chrome fragments in it, bald, ridged, theatrical, and he talks to the player the way the lore's framing talks: profane, sardonic, fond of his own jokes, with contempt for the Oathkeepers that never becomes a speech. He is a D4 who cannot leave the Mat; the Runners go where he cannot, and he narrates their runs from the Heart over the comm (barks during runs are his, pitched to the Runner's situation). The rules below are the `story` crate's, its data `data/chapters.toml` and `data/barks.toml` (PAC-081, research 22).

**On the map** (Pass 1's four verbs, Pass 2's hero unit). Ivan walks the Mat only — Tip, Cord and Node and the structures on them — a tile every two ticks as a slimo does, HP 260 (a Runner's 130 twice: a D4's body; this design's), mended to full at each shift's end, and stands at the Heart where nothing is ordered; the squads find him among the city's slimos. Select him by his portrait on the HUD or on the map; right-click a tile of the Mat to send him. His card carries the verbs, each once a shift:

## Technical architecture and repository

Rust owns every rule and every number; Godot owns the clock, the input, the camera and the picture; the asset system sits between them so art can land without touching either.

```markdown
rose/
  core/                      Rust workspace (no engine dependency; headless-testable)
    crates/mat               CA + nutrient field, organs, strains, signal       (M1, re-gridded to 32)
    crates/logistics         the city's clock (Mat step every 12 ticks), consumer fields (signal-weighted), cargo packets with capacity and re-routing, jams, the roster's movers; the scale proof
    crates/items             item table, instances, condition, identification, pricing, sets, crafted stats and provenance
    crates/bench             recipes by craft tag (data/recipes.toml), slotting, outcome from parts, the Bench queue
    crates/people            slimo records: creation, stats, skills and XP, traits (data/traits.toml), needs and Mood, job utility, bonds, the roster
    crates/inventory         grids, containers, rigs, Stash tiers
```

**Asset slots.** A drawable in Godot references `AssetSlot("slimo.d2.walk")`. The loader resolves `assets/art/<id>.png` if present and validated, else `assets/placeholders/<id>.png`. The manifest entry carries size, frame count, state tiers, the anchor to match and the PixelLab prompt, so the prompt sheet for the artist is generated from the same file the engine reads. A slot cannot be referenced that is not in the manifest (build fails), and a manifest entry cannot lack a placeholder (tool regenerates them).

**Data over code.** Items, chunks, loot tables, creatures, recipes, structures, laws, traits, barks, vendors and tech are data files with schema validation; balancing never recompiles. Chunks are authored as glyph grids (`tools/gen/gen_chunks.py` writes `data/chunks.toml`); a Godot tile-editor export to the same format lands with Phase F tooling.

**Determinism.** One PCG32 stream per sim; UI and VFX draw from a second; a save replays from seed and input log. The run is `run::sim::RunSim`: every rule of a run — movement, senses, loot, creatures, followers, combat, digging, extraction, the outcome — advances in `step(input)` at 20 Hz on integer and fixed-point state, the input word is logged (`Input::RunInput`), and Godot's Run screen is a view that gathers input and draws state and events. Save format v3 carries `route_memory` per (seed, stratum) and `corpses` (route, tile, day, the rig); migrations begin with the first shipped save format. `run::board::RouteCard` computes a card from the compiled route, the creature file and the route's memory bits, so the Board never counts anything itself; `RunSim` carries the shift clock (`shift_ticks`, overtime spawns from `[shift]` in `data/creatures.toml`). The Stash is one `RoseStash` held by the `Sim` autoload for the whole session; screens are views of it. `inventory::ledger` holds the sell and feed queues, the eddies and per-market glut, and resolves a shift into a report (eddies earned, deliveries of nutrient and genome drift per Pit) that the colony applies; save v4 carries the ledger. The `bench` crate loads `data/recipes.toml`, validates a slotting, computes the outcome (an `ItemInstance` with `crafted` provenance and `mods` over its def) and runs the Bench queue to shift end; save v5 carries the queue and the instance fields. The `people` crate owns the slimo record (creation from the seed, the forty traits from `data/traits.toml`, names from `data/names.toml`, needs and Mood per shift, XP from the run outcome and the shift report, job utility, bonds) in a `Roster`; save v6 carries it, the Route Board reads its eligible slimos and the run takes a `RunnerSetup` (body, nerve, hands, eye, veins, rig rows, the run flags) from the record.

**Tests.** Every crate has scenario tests (seeded, asserting regimes); the `render` crate emits a contact sheet per module that the differ compares per commit; the Godot smoke test exercises every mode shell headless. A pull request that changes a contact sheet carries the diff image.
